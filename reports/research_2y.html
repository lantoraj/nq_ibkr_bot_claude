On two years of data the edge mostly disappears.

The strategy that earned +$1,789 over the 66 most recent sessions (all the data we had at first) earns +$1,538 over 517 sessions (about $750 a year on one MNQ), with a profit factor of 1.05 and a Sharpe of 0.36. The deflated Sharpe is 0.20. This is not a strategy to trade with real money as it stands.

What the two years show

  • The 66-session window was the best stretch. Outside it the strategy roughly broke even (about −$250 over the other ~450 sessions; approximate, since indicator warm-up differs). The first year lost $673 (profit factor 0.95); the second year made $2,211.
  • Only 13 of 25 months were profitable, and 204 of 517 days. Expectancy is 0.007 R per trade, essentially zero.
  • Profit is still concentrated in a few days. The top 3 days make 93% of net profit; without the best 5 days the strategy loses $845.
  • The edge is thinner than the cost uncertainty. At double commission net falls to +$402; with 2 ticks of worse entry, +$838; at 3 ticks of stop slippage, +$781; all three together lose $1,055.
  • Tuning does not generalise. Walk-forward re-optimisation made +$854 against +$1,624 for the fixed defaults on the same windows. Reward-to-risk 2.0 loses $422. Across the ATR-multiple by reward-to-risk grid, results range from −$2,068 to +$3,775, so the defaults are not special.
  • Several components now hurt. Removing the confidence gate (+$1,932), the PDH/PDL guard (+$2,072) or the time-decay stop (+$1,864) each raises net profit. Pullback-only entries make +$3,500 (profit factor 1.13, drawdown $1,384, 15 of 25 months): the crossover, momentum and opening-range setups are net drags. The VWAP z-band still helps (without it: +$525).
  • It still beats random entries, modestly. +$1,538 against a random-entry 95th percentile of +$730 (97th percentile); 17% of random runs are profitable. There is some entry-selection value, but not much.
  • Statistically unconvincing. Probabilistic Sharpe vs zero is 0.70; at this Sharpe about 5,000 trading days (20 years) would be needed for 95% confidence. After deflating for 90 variants tried, luck alone is expected to produce a Sharpe of 0.95, which is above the observed 0.36.
  • Drawdown risk is larger than the single path suggests. Bootstrapping the trades gives a 30% chance that a sample like this nets zero or less, and a 95th-percentile maximum drawdown of $5,134 per MNQ.
  • Last report's findings reversed. Entry options flipped sign between samples (structural stop: 2-bar was best on 66 days, 8-bar is best now; a 0.10 ATR retrace: +$3,630 now, but it lost in the first half). The confirmation bar still destroys the edge. That instability is the sign of noise, so none of these are adopted.

What I'd do next

  1. No live money. Treat the strategy as unvalidated.
  2. Simplify and freeze, then test forward. Pullback-only is the best of the 90 variants tried, which is exactly why its result is suspect. Fix its rules now and paper trade 8–12 weeks; only data it has never seen can tell.
  3. Fix the event calendar. It covers only 4 of 25 months, so the event-day and context findings below are unreliable until it covers the whole period.
  4. Test the overnight guard. This data includes overnight bars, but this run did not test it.
  5. Measure real slippage on paper. The result flips sign inside plausible cost assumptions.

Reading this. With 90 variants tried, luck alone is expected to produce an annualised Sharpe of about 0.95; the default's is 0.36, so its deflated Sharpe is 0.20 (not convincing). The count N is a lower bound on the real number of decisions behind the defaults (the original author's v0-v4 tuning is not counted), and variants that differ slightly are correlated, which pulls the effective N down; treat the DSR as a guide, not a p-value.
